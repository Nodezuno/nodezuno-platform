# Nodezuno Partnerships and Grants

**Status:** Proposed commercial and partner operating model

**Document version:** 0.1

**Published:** 6 August 2026

## Truth boundary

Nodezuno has not established a grant award, named protocol partnership,
customer deployment, paid chain-launch engagement, recurring infrastructure
contract, published price, or revenue through this repository.

A form submission is an inquiry. A meeting is a discussion. A proposal is an
offer to scope work. A grant becomes an award only through the grant program's
formal decision and controlling documents. A commercial relationship becomes
a customer relationship only through an executed agreement.

## Partner thesis

New networks need infrastructure before they have mature usage revenue.
Protocol foundations and ecosystem programs often fund public goods that make
a network usable, including RPC, explorers, faucets, snapshots,
documentation, monitoring, and testnet operations.

Nodezuno intends to propose infrastructure scopes with objective milestones so
that a foundation, core team, or ecosystem fund can evaluate concrete outputs
instead of funding an undefined platform promise.

## Qualified partner profiles

### Protocol foundation

An organization authorized to fund and approve public infrastructure for a
network.

### Core protocol team

The team responsible for client software, releases, genesis, network identity,
and technical acceptance.

### Rollup, L2, or appchain team

An authorized team launching a dedicated network or execution environment with
clear ownership of its stack and public surfaces.

### Ecosystem fund

An authorized program that can fund measurable public infrastructure and name
the protocol team responsible for technical acceptance.

### Infrastructure migration partner

An existing network with authority to replace or diversify nodes, RPC,
explorers, data, snapshots, or operational tooling.

## Qualification information

A useful network brief contains:

- network and organization name;
- authorized contact and role;
- website, repository, and technical documentation;
- L1, L2, rollup, appchain, or other stack classification;
- execution and consensus clients;
- current devnet, testnet, mainnet, or migration stage;
- target public-testnet and mainnet dates;
- required infrastructure surfaces;
- existing providers and dependencies;
- validator and key ownership;
- expected regions, traffic, storage, and developer audience;
- grant program or infrastructure RFP link;
- proposal and decision dates;
- funding or procurement constraints the partner chooses to disclose; and
- known technical, legal, or operational blockers.

Do not submit private keys, seed phrases, cloud credentials, customer data, or
confidential exploit details through a public intake form.

## Proposed engagement models

### 1. Network specification engagement

Candidate output:

- versioned network manifest;
- infrastructure topology;
- responsibility and dependency map;
- threat and failure analysis;
- testnet or mainnet launch plan;
- evidence and acceptance gates; and
- estimate with explicit assumptions and exclusions.

This can be a paid discovery scope or an approved grant milestone. It should
not promise deployment before requirements and authority are clear.

### 2. Milestone-based protocol grant

Candidate public-good outputs:

- public-testnet RPC;
- deterministic block explorer;
- faucet and abuse controls;
- signed snapshots and restore guidance;
- client, peer, and endpoint documentation;
- separate execution, consensus, and data status;
- testnet upgrade and recovery evidence; and
- open-source adapters or runbooks where the agreement requires them.

Grant proposals must name which outputs are public goods, open source,
partner-specific, or operated only during the funded period.

### 3. Paid launch implementation

Candidate scope:

- private devnet;
- public-testnet infrastructure;
- chain-specific RPC adapter;
- deterministic index and explorer;
- snapshot and restore process;
- monitoring and status;
- upgrade rehearsal;
- mainnet readiness review; and
- agreed launch support.

### 4. Recurring managed infrastructure

Candidate recurring responsibilities:

- declared node roles;
- RPC gateway and capacity;
- index and explorer operation;
- consensus observations without assumed key custody;
- snapshots and restore checks;
- client upgrades;
- status and incident communication;
- capacity and cost review; and
- periodic evidence revalidation.

Coverage hours, response targets, regions, recovery objectives, and service
credits require an executed agreement and operational evidence.

### 5. Usage-based developer infrastructure

RPC, indexed data, or explorer APIs may eventually be metered. The current
repository has quota and usage primitives but no paid billing for the proposed
chain-launch products.

### 6. Dedicated capacity or explorer license

A partner may request isolated node capacity or a chain-branded explorer.
Scope depends on software, storage, regions, traffic, data retention,
customization, support, and legal obligations.

## Milestone template

Every proposed milestone should state:

1. **Authorized network:** exact network and environment.
2. **Objective:** the outcome the partner is funding.
3. **Deliverables:** artifacts, services, documents, or exercises.
4. **Exclusions:** work and guarantees outside the milestone.
5. **Dependencies:** partner, Nodezuno, and third-party inputs.
6. **Ownership:** infrastructure, code, data, keys, domains, and accounts.
7. **Acceptance evidence:** exact tests, records, and approvers.
8. **Security boundary:** authority, credentials, data, and key handling.
9. **Public obligations:** open-source, reporting, attribution, and public-good terms.
10. **Schedule:** target dates and conditions that can change them.
11. **Payment:** controlling grant or commercial terms.
12. **Correction and termination:** failure, withdrawal, and transition path.

## Suggested grant sequence

### Milestone A: specification

Deliver:

- network manifest;
- topology;
- responsibility map;
- interface policies;
- launch gates; and
- unresolved-risk record.

Evidence:

- approved versioned artifacts;
- named partner technical approver; and
- accepted exclusions and dependencies.

### Milestone B: private devnet

Deliver:

- reproducible declared node roles;
- exact chain probes;
- replacement and recovery;
- minimal RPC and deterministic indexing; and
- one upgrade rehearsal.

Evidence:

- chain identity;
- client and artifact compatibility;
- replacement and restore records;
- upgrade result; and
- partner review.

### Milestone C: public testnet

Deliver:

- bounded RPC;
- deterministic explorer;
- faucet if applicable;
- signed snapshots;
- developer documentation;
- separate execution, consensus, and data status; and
- support and incident paths.

Evidence:

- security review;
- capacity and abuse test;
- data canonicality and lag;
- snapshot restore;
- failure exercise; and
- partner acceptance.

### Milestone D: mainnet readiness

Deliver:

- production manifest;
- capacity and storage plan;
- key and access review;
- upgrade and disaster exercises;
- status, incident, and escalation runbooks; and
- launch decision package.

Evidence:

- dated gate record;
- unresolved-risk register;
- protocol approval; and
- applicable commercial and legal scope.

### Milestone E: managed operations

Deliver:

- recurring declared infrastructure;
- client and dependency updates;
- capacity and cost review;
- incidents and post-incident actions;
- snapshot and recovery checks; and
- evidence revalidation.

Evidence:

- operating record for the agreed period; and
- partner review against the executed scope.

## Acceptance evidence rules

Evidence should include:

- network, environment, and manifest version;
- date, duration, and owner;
- clients, versions, artifacts, and checksums;
- regions and material dependencies;
- exact test method and workload;
- expected and observed result;
- raw evidence or durable location;
- limitations and unresolved risks;
- approver; and
- expiry or revalidation trigger.

Screenshots, a polished demo, a green process check, or a repository link are
not sufficient by themselves.

## Revenue recognition boundary

Public product copy should not use revenue, annual recurring revenue, booked,
funded, customer, or grant recipient based on:

- an inquiry;
- a meeting;
- a memorandum without the relevant commitment;
- a grant application;
- a non-binding proposal;
- promised ecosystem credits;
- infrastructure tested without an executed relationship; or
- an invoice that is not collectible under the controlling terms.

Accounting treatment requires the executed agreement, performance
obligations, delivery and acceptance state, collectibility, and applicable
professional review. This document is not accounting advice.

## Public partnership claims

Before publishing a partner name, logo, quotation, grant amount, deployment,
or result:

- obtain written approval for the exact wording and asset;
- identify the relationship and environment;
- state whether it is a grant, customer, design partner, evaluation, or public
  infrastructure contribution;
- verify dates, amounts, deliverables, and acceptance;
- remove confidential architecture and security details;
- avoid implying protocol or ecosystem endorsement beyond the approved scope;
- retain the approver and withdrawal path; and
- set a review date.

## Pricing principles

A future price depends on:

- environments and protocol stack;
- node roles and counts;
- clouds, regions, and failure independence;
- compute, storage, bandwidth, and state growth;
- RPC methods, throughput, concurrency, and support;
- index, search, retention, and replay;
- explorer and AI scope;
- consensus and upgrade responsibilities;
- recovery and service objectives;
- security and key responsibilities;
- launch schedule and staffing; and
- agreement term and legal obligations.

Public pricing must state currency, unit, period, taxes, included usage,
overage, effective date, availability, and exclusions. No public chain-launch
price is active today.

## Partner intake outcome

A successful intake should promise only:

> Nodezuno stored the network brief and can use it to prepare a scoped launch
> review, responsibility map, and evidence checklist.

It must not promise selection, funding, a response time, deployment, capacity,
or launch.
